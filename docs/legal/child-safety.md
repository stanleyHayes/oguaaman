These are the published child safety standards of Oguaa, the community platform for Cape Coast, for the Oguaa websites and the Oguaa mobile app on the App Store and Google Play.

## Zero tolerance

Oguaa has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), including child sexual abuse material (CSAM). We never allow content, accounts or behaviour that sexualises, grooms, exploits, trafficks or endangers a child. This applies to images, video, text, links, profiles and messages sent through Oguaa, whether real or computer-generated.

## Who can use Oguaa

Oguaa accounts are for adults aged 18 and over. Everyone confirms their age when they join, and we remove accounts that belong to children. Oguaa has no private messaging or chat between members. The one exception is the lost & found relay: a signed-in member can send a single screened message to the person who posted a lost & found notice. Oguaa delivers it with the sender's name, there is no reply thread, and the poster's contact details are never shown. Youth and mentorship listings are public programme notices, and they must follow our [Safeguarding Policy](/safeguarding).

## How to report a concern

- **In the app or on the web:** use Report on the listing, profile, tribute, review, news article or agent, and choose **Child safety**. You need to be signed in. Reported listings, tributes, reviews, news articles and agent profiles are hidden from public view immediately, and every child-safety report goes to the top of our moderators' queue.
- **By email:** write to [hello@oguaaman.com](mailto:hello@oguaaman.com) with "Child safety" in the subject line and a link to what you saw. You don't need an account.
- **Without an account:** you can also use the [request form](/privacy/request).
- **If a child is in immediate danger,** contact the Ghana Police Service or emergency services first.

Please don't copy, screenshot or forward abusive images of children, even to report them. Send us the link instead.

## What we do

1. **Remove:** child-safety reports hide the content straight away wherever it can be hidden. When a moderator confirms abuse or exploitation, we remove it permanently. We act on every child-safety report within 24 hours.
2. **Preserve evidence:** we keep a restricted copy of the reported content and the account details, so the authorities can investigate. Only authorised staff can access it.
3. **Close accounts:** we suspend and permanently close accounts that post, share or seek such content.
4. **Report to the authorities:** we report CSAM and child exploitation to the Ghana Police Service and the Cyber Security Authority under the Cybersecurity Act, 2020 (Act 1038), and to other national or international authorities where the law requires it or where it helps protect a child, such as the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC).
5. **Co-operate:** we respond to lawful requests from law enforcement investigating harm to children.

## Missing and vulnerable children

Only a parent, guardian or close relative, or a teacher or official acting with the family's knowledge, can post a notice about a missing child. They must confirm their relationship to the child, and a curator checks the notice before it is published. We take the notice down when the child is marked as found. We never show contact details on a public notice: replies go through Oguaa's relay.

## Keeping these standards

Our moderators treat child-safety reports as their top priority, and an automated screen holds posts that combine references to children with sexual language, so a person reviews them before anyone else sees them. We review these standards at least once a year and whenever the law or the app stores' requirements change.
