These standards cover the news published on Oguaa: on the websites (oguaaman.com and citizen.oguaaman.com) and in the Oguaa app. They explain the kinds of news we publish, how we use AI to help, the human checks that come before anything AI-assisted is published, and what to do if we get something wrong.

## Kinds of news on Oguaa

### Articles by people

Articles written by Oguaa editors, members and verified local authorities carry their author's name. They follow our [Terms of Use](/terms) and are reviewed as the terms describe.

### Briefs

The newsroom follows public news feeds from trusted Ghanaian publishers and local authorities. A brief is a short summary of a story, at most 60 words, based only on the teaser the publisher puts in its feed, never on the full article. Every brief names the publisher and, when known, the original author, and links to the full story.

- A brief taken from the feed is labelled "Automated summary of a trusted public source" and may be published automatically.
- A brief reworded by AI is labelled "AI-generated summary of a trusted public source" and waits for an editor before it is published.
- A brief about politics or an election always waits for an editor.
- We skip publishers whose feeds reserve all rights, unless we have a licence from them.

### AI-assisted reports

Some briefs become fuller reports of about 300 to 700 words. Claude, an AI system made by Anthropic, searches the web and reads pages from a fixed list of Ghanaian news and official sites, and drafts the report from what it finds.

- Every report draws on at least two different publishers.
- Facts are tied to numbered sources listed under the article, with the original report marked.
- Automatic checks reject a draft that copies a source's wording, quotes too much, cites too little or has too few sources.
- When an editor approves a report, it replaces the brief at the same address.

Every report carries this label, with the editor's name: "AI-assisted report: drafted by AI from the sources listed below and reviewed by [editor] before publication. Oguaa, operated by Dev Track, is responsible for this article." A report about politics or an election adds: "This is election coverage: an editor checked it for balance."

## An editor reviews every AI-assisted report

- **No AI-written report is ever published automatically.** There is no setting that changes this.
- An editor reads every draft against its sources before it is published. They can edit it, ask for a new draft or reject it.
- Before approving, the editor confirms a checklist: the facts match the sources, the report is fair and, for political stories, balanced, and any illustration follows the rules below.
- The editor's name, or their public byline, appears in the report's label.
- We don't send push notifications for AI-assisted reports.

## Topics AI never drafts

We don't use AI to write reports on these topics. Every story is screened for them before any drafting and again before an editor sees a draft. A story that touches them stays a short brief, and any AI draft of it is discarded. Political briefs always wait for an editor:

- allegations of crime or wrongdoing about named people;
- court cases;
- security incidents and conflict;
- chieftaincy disputes;
- disease outbreaks and medical claims;
- stories involving children;
- election procedures and results;
- any political story while election mode is on.

## Election coverage

Election mode is switched on around elections, from Oguaa's election calendar or by an editor. While it is on, AI drafts no political story and no political story gets an AI illustration. At all times, political reports are checked for balance by an editor and labelled as election coverage, and political briefs wait for an editor.

## Images and illustrations

- **AI illustrations are labelled.** Some reports are illustrated with an image drawn by OpenAI's image model. It carries an "AI illustration" label and the caption "AI illustration generated with OpenAI for Oguaa. It does not show the real people, place or event." Its description for screen readers starts "AI illustration:".
- **Never a photograph, never a real person.** Illustrations are drawn in a clearly illustrated style from a short description of a calm scene, such as a market, a shoreline or a classroom. They never show real or recognisable people, and they contain no text, logos, flags, party symbols, weapons, injuries or disaster scenes.
- **Political and sensitive stories use a newsroom graphic.** Stories about politics or elections, stories on the topics above, and every story during election mode get an Oguaa Newsroom graphic (the headline on Oguaa's colours) instead of an AI illustration.
- **Provenance is kept.** We store each AI illustration as the provider delivered it, including any content credentials the provider adds, and tag it as AI-generated.
- Photos taken by people are credited to the photographer or the source.

## Sources, quotes and copyright

- We report facts in our own words. Under the Copyright Act, 2005 (Act 690), facts are free to report but the way a writer expresses them is not.
- A report may quote at most two short passages of up to 25 words each, always in quotation marks and credited.
- Every source is listed and linked. The pages the AI reads are used only to draft the report and are not stored.
- If you think we have used your work unfairly, email [hello@oguaaman.com](mailto:hello@oguaaman.com) with "Copyright" in the subject line, the article's link and the work concerned. We act on notices promptly and remove material that infringes.

## Corrections and right of reply

If we get something wrong, we correct it openly. Corrections are dated and shown on the article. We don't quietly change a published fact.

Under Article 162(6) of the Constitution, if we publish a statement about you, you have the right to have your reply (a rejoinder) published. To ask for a correction or to send a reply:

1. Use "Report" on the article, or the [request form](/privacy/request), or email [hello@oguaaman.com](mailto:hello@oguaaman.com) with "Correction" or "Reply" in the subject line.
2. Include the article's link, what is wrong or what you want to say, and how we can reach you.
3. We respond **within 24 hours**. We publish corrections and rejoinders free of charge, with the article they concern.

## Advertising and independence

Advertisers and sponsors have no say over what we report. Ads are labelled, styled differently from news and never placed inside an article's text. See our [Advertising Policy](/advertising).

## AI systems we use

In line with the Paris Charter on AI and Journalism, we list every AI system the newsroom uses:

- **Claude Opus (Anthropic), with Anthropic's web search and web fetch tools:** researches leads and drafts AI-assisted reports, then prepares the headline, summary and topic tags and flags sensitive topics for the editor.
- **Claude Haiku (Anthropic):** rewords some briefs, which are then labelled and held for an editor as described above.
- **GPT Image (OpenAI):** draws the AI illustrations described above.

Our automatic checks (source counts, copying and quotation limits, and the topic screens) are rules written by Oguaa, not AI. No member data is sent to an AI system for news. We will update this list before the newsroom starts using a new AI system.
